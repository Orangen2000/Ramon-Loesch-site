Schimäre, 2026

The word chimera means illusion. It is an imaginary phenomenon that does not correspond to reality. The video recordings date from 2023 and were made in an art foundry in Mainz-Kastel, which closed in early 2024.

The work is always projected freely into spaces, independent of objects in the room, slanted walls, or corners. The lighting conditions can also vary naturally, sometimes making the projection almost invisible for short periods.

In der Nacht länger klar, 2025

Maße variabel

Video (28.15 min), Monitor, Kabel, Print, Plexiglas, Acrylfarbe, MDF-Platten, Nägel

My Hometown Is My Hometown Is — A pet street market in Seoul, a friend’s impending breakup, two flocks of geese swimming towards each other on the coast of England and then merging into a group, the call to prayer from the minaret of a mosque in Seoul, a Christmas market stall with around 50 music boxes playing in parallel in Vienna, a chemical factory in Mannheim by day and by night, the story of a disused airport that was sold for one pound and is now a reception center for refugees, a drone circling a fairground in Incheon, and so on.

Two images, two possibilities, two perspectives, is one image, one sound, one video.

16 Tage Vorhersage (Offenbach) — Printed weather forecasts are visible behind Plexiglas. They show 16 daily forecasts for the exhibition period—each from a different day. The forecasts vary from one another and rarely, if ever, correspond to the actual weather at the time of the exhibition.

Ophiocordyceps unilateralis, 2025

432 × 174 × 163 cm

Opel Zafira, Kreidespray, Video mit Sound, Monitore, Kabel

Ophiocordyceps unilateralis is the name of a parasitic fungus that grows on ants and manipulates their behavior so that the host has no control over its own body.

In a meadow stands a car—a gray Opel Zafira with windows painted white on the inside. Small, circular areas are left unpainted. Sounds emanate from the car: an indefinable hiss, a smacking, a grinding, or a metallic rustling. A black cable hangs from the driver’s side window, snaking its way across the meadow from a house to the car, feeding two opposing monitors in the trunk. Approaching the car and looking through one of the small, unpainted peepholes, one sees a black-and-white video playing inside.

Ant bodies, seemingly engaged in combat, merge together.

Starting with a BBC documentary about ant warfare, I embarked on a process whose outcome was uncertain. I worked with AI as both a tool and a partner. First, I copied the documentary from YouTube and deleted 20 of the 21 frames. Then, I had the AI regenerate the missing frames. This resulted in a new version of the same video—a completely different version in which the ants merge instead of disintegrating.

Widerstand, 2023

Videoinstallation, 19:36 min

For the work “Resistance” I projected scans of newspaper photographs about the protest against the West Runway in the 80s onto the outer wall of Frankfurt Airport, where the protest took place. The car drives continuously around the airport. You can hear the deep hum of the car, and every now and then a plane flies over the scene. The images are sometimes more clearly visible, sometimes they disappear completely. The newspapers come from a collection of my grandfather, who actively took part in the protests.
